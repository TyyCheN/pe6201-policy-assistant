# Chapter 2 — Leaving the Company

## 1 Notice of resignation

[D02-1.1] An employee who resigns in the ordinary way shall give the company 30 days' written notice; during the probation period, 3 days' notice. The two parties may agree on an earlier leaving date. Where the law allows an employee to terminate the employment contract with immediate effect, the law applies.
[D02-1.2] An ordinary resignation notice is a notice, and it does not need the company's approval to take effect. After receiving the notice, the company shall promptly confirm the expected leaving date, the handover items and the person taking over, and must not delay the process without good reason.
[D02-1.3] Consecutive absence without leave, or being out of contact, is not treated as an automatic voluntary resignation, and the employment contract is not terminated automatically because a number of days of absence has been reached. The company shall verify the reason, try to contact the employee and send a formal reminder, and handle the matter under Chapter 3 and Chapter 9.

## 2 Handover

[D02-2.1] The employee shall, as agreed by both parties and within a reasonable period, hand over work tasks, production or customer information, documents, equipment, tools, keys, authorised accounts and other company property, and a handover list signed by both parties shall be drawn up.
[D02-2.2] The department head shall designate the person taking over and provide the conditions for the handover. The employee shall truthfully settle expense claims, repay borrowed funds and explain unfinished matters and risks; where a personal account is involved, authorisation shall be migrated or a similar method used, and the employee is not required to hand over personal passwords unrelated to work.
[D02-2.3] A leaving audit may be arranged for key positions, but disputes over the audit or the handover must not become a reason to delay the termination procedure indefinitely or to withhold all wages. An unverified loss must not be used directly as the basis for a deduction.
[D02-2.4] Where an employee, intentionally or through gross negligence, refuses to hand over and thereby causes actual loss, the company may claim compensation according to law. Ordinary recruitment and onboarding training costs must not be charged as a penalty for leaving; liability for a special-training service period and for non-compete obligations is handled according to law and the agreement.

## 3 Final settlement and certificate

[D02-3.1] After the employment relationship is lawfully terminated or ends, the company shall, under Jiangsu rules, pay all wages in one lump sum within two working days; where the two parties have another lawful agreement, that agreement applies. The settlement shall itemise wages, overtime pay, pay for untaken annual leave payable by law, bonuses and other items due.
[D02-3.2] Severance pay payable by law is handled under the law and in light of the handover. The company shall issue a certificate when the employment contract is terminated or ends, and shall complete the transfer of the personnel file and social insurance within the statutory time limit; it must not use wages or the certificate as a condition for forcing the employee to give up rights.
[D02-3.3] After leaving, the employee must still keep confidential the trade secrets that are protected by law. A post-employment non-compete obligation applies only to the categories of personnel permitted by law and requires a separate written agreement on its scope, territory, duration and compensation; this handbook must not be used to require all employees to bear a post-employment non-compete obligation without compensation.
