# Chapter 7 — Leave

## 1 Application and approval

[D07-1.1] To take leave, an employee shall submit an application on paper or in an electronic form accepted by the company, stating the type of leave, the start and end time, the necessary reason and the handover items. The company collects only the supporting documents needed for review and protects the employee's privacy.
[D07-1.2] Leave of no more than 2 hours is approved by the direct supervisor; leave of more than 2 hours but no more than 1 normal working day is approved by the Administration & HR Department after seeking the supervisor's opinion; leave of more than 1 normal working day is approved by the General Manager or a person authorised by the General Manager in writing, and filed with the Administration & HR Department. The approval level is determined by the cumulative working time covered by the continuous application, and an application is not to be split in order to avoid the approval level.
[D07-1.3] For statutory leave, the approver checks the conditions and arranges the handover, and must not refuse leave to which the employee is entitled by law on the ground that production is busy, that there is no one to cover, or that it is at the company's discretion. The approver shall respond promptly and must not delay without good reason.
[D07-1.4] In case of sudden illness, an accident or another emergency where leave cannot be applied for in advance, the employee shall notify the supervisor and the Administration & HR Department as soon as he or she is able to make contact, in principle before the start of the shift; where the employee cannot make contact personally for objective reasons such as emergency treatment or being unreachable, a family member may make contact on the employee's behalf. The paperwork is completed within 2 working days after returning to work; where an objective obstacle remains, the period is reasonably extended after an explanation, and the absence is not treated directly as absence without leave.
[D07-1.5] An extension of leave shall be applied for before the original leave ends; where a sudden cause makes it impossible to apply in advance, the preceding clause applies. Within 2 working days after returning to work, the employee completes the return-from-leave report and the check of the leave actually taken; no fine is imposed merely for failing to file the return-from-leave report.
[D07-1.6] The supervisor is responsible for arranging cover and the handover. The employee shall cooperate reasonably, but the employee's failure to find a substitute personally is not a reason to deny leave that has already been approved.

## 2 Statutory public holidays and annual leave

[D07-2.1] Statutory public holidays follow the national rules and the holiday arrangement for the year; working days swapped under the holiday arrangement follow the published schedule.
[D07-2.2] An employee who has worked continuously for 12 months is entitled by law to paid annual leave: 5 days for a cumulative working life of at least 1 year but less than 10 years, 10 days for at least 10 years but less than 20 years, and 15 days for 20 years or more. Cumulative working life includes work for other employers that by law must be counted together, and is not limited to service with this company.
[D07-2.3] Annual leave in the year of joining and in the year of leaving is pro-rated under the national rules; statutory public holidays and rest days are not counted as annual leave. The company plans leave taking account of production and the employee's wishes, and may arrange it in one block or in several parts; it must not force annual leave to be used to offset other statutory leave.
[D07-2.4] Where annual leave truly cannot be arranged because of work needs, the employee's consent shall be obtained and pay for the untaken annual leave shall be paid according to law. Where the 300% standard applies by law, it includes the wage for normal work; where the normal wage has already been paid, a further 200% is paid. Where an employee, for personal reasons, states in writing that he or she will not take annual leave that has been arranged, the matter is handled according to law.
[D07-2.5] The cases in which an employee is by law not entitled to annual leave for the year, the effect of cumulative sick and personal leave, carry-over across years and the pro-rating method follow the Regulations on Paid Annual Leave for Employees and the Implementation Measures for Paid Annual Leave for Enterprise Employees, and the Administration & HR Department explains the basis and the calculation to the employees concerned.

## 3 Personal leave

[D07-3.1] An employee who truly needs time off for personal matters may apply for personal leave, which is unpaid and is calculated on the actual time taken. In principle it does not exceed 2 working days a month, 15 working days a year and 5 consecutive working days on any one occasion; where there is a reasonable special cause, the General Manager or an authorised person may approve an exception.
[D07-3.2] These limits apply only to personal leave and do not apply to sick leave, the medical treatment period, work injury or other statutory leave. Exceeding the limit does not automatically turn the leave into absence without leave or lead to termination of the contract; the approval outcome shall first be made clear and the employee given a reasonable opportunity to communicate.
[D07-3.3] Where a long continuous period of personal leave truly requires a special arrangement, the two parties may agree in writing on its duration, the return to work, social insurance and other matters, but an arrangement called unpaid leave of absence must not be used to stop statutory social insurance obligations or to replace the medical treatment period, maternity leave or work-injury benefits. The return to work at the end of the period and the handling of the employment relationship follow the law; the employee is not treated as having resigned automatically, nor is the contract terminated on the ground that there is no position.

## 4 Sick leave and the medical treatment period

[D07-4.1] An employee who needs rest and treatment because of illness or a non-work-related injury applies for sick leave with the necessary proof, such as a diagnosis or sick-leave recommendation issued by a lawful medical institution; hospitalisation, or treatment only at a local city-level hospital, is not a condition for all sick leave. Emergency treatment and treatment in another city are verified according to the actual circumstances.
[D07-4.2] The company's existing benefit is retained: up to 2 normal working days of verified sick leave per month are paid at the wage for normal working time; this allowance is not carried over and is not paid out in cash, and less than one day is counted by the actual hours. Sick leave beyond this benefit is handled under the rules on the statutory medical treatment period and sick pay, and is not the same as unpaid leave.
[D07-4.3] The statutory medical treatment period is determined by the employee's actual cumulative years of work and years of service with this company, is generally 3 to 24 months, and is calculated cumulatively as the law provides; special illnesses and cases where the law allows an extension follow the applicable rules. There is no limit of a maximum of 30 days of sick leave a year, and no rule that pay must stop for sick leave of more than ten days.
[D07-4.4] During the medical treatment period, sick pay or sickness relief is paid according to law, specifically under the lawful pay system, the employment contract, the collective contract and national and Jiangsu rules, and not less than 80% of the local minimum wage; where payment is made at that floor, the company also bears, according to law, the social insurance contributions and housing fund contributions that the employee would personally pay. Where the employment contract or a valid rule provides for higher treatment, that provision applies.
[D07-4.5] Where, at the end of the medical treatment period, the employee still needs treatment or cannot return to work, the medical opinion, the employee's capacity for work and the positions that can be arranged shall be verified and the matter handled according to law; the contract is not terminated automatically. Absence during a lawful medical treatment period must not be counted as absence without leave or used as the basis for disciplinary action.

## 5 Marriage leave and bereavement leave

[D07-5.1] An employee who registers a marriage according to law is entitled, under Jiangsu rules, to 13 days of marriage leave, with no additional condition of late marriage age or first marriage; statutory public holidays are not counted, and rest days are counted under the applicable rules. The employee applies promptly with proof of marriage registration, and the leave is in principle arranged within one year of registration; where there is a reasonable cause, or the delay is caused by the company's arrangements, another time is agreed and the leave is not cancelled mechanically.
[D07-5.2] Where an employee's spouse, parent, child or parent-in-law dies, 3 days of paid bereavement leave are given. Where the employee has to travel to another place for the funeral, additional travel leave is given according to the reasonable distance; transport costs are borne by the employee, unless a more favourable arrangement has been agreed.
[D07-5.3] Bereavement leave is in principle taken consecutively at the time of the death; where there are special funeral arrangements, the two parties agree reasonably, and the leave is not refused across the board on the ground that more than one week has passed since the death.

## 6 Maternity leave and childbirth-related leave

[D07-6.1] A female employee who gives birth is entitled to the 98 days of maternity leave provided by the State; where the birth complies with Jiangsu rules, she is entitled to a further 60 days of extended maternity leave, 158 days in total. A difficult delivery adds 15 days; for a multiple birth, 15 days are added for each additional baby. 15 days of the maternity leave may be taken before the birth as provided.
[D07-6.2] The 60 days of Jiangsu extended maternity leave do not count statutory public holidays; the basic maternity leave provided by the State is counted under its own rules. The day-counting rules for basic maternity leave and extended maternity leave must not be mixed up.
[D07-6.3] For a miscarriage at less than 2 months of pregnancy, not less than 20 days of maternity leave are given; at 2 months or more but less than 3 months, not less than 30 days; for a miscarriage or induced labour at 3 months or more but less than 7 months, not less than 42 days; for induced labour at 7 months or more, not less than 98 days. The leave is processed on the basis of a certificate from a medical institution, and any more favourable treatment that applies by law is followed.
[D07-6.4] Pay and the maternity allowance during maternity leave and related leave are handled together under national and Jiangsu rules. Where the maternity allowance is lower than the pre-maternity-leave wage standard that applies by law, the company makes up the difference; where it is higher, the company does not keep the excess, and the amount is not halved because the employee has worked for less than one year. The company's statutory payment responsibility is not removed because the employee was not enrolled in insurance.
[D07-6.5] A male employee who meets Jiangsu rules is entitled to 15 days of paternity (nursing) leave during his spouse's maternity leave; statutory public holidays are not counted, and holding an only-child certificate is not a condition.
[D07-6.6] For employees who meet Jiangsu rules, until the child reaches 3 years of age each parent is entitled to 10 days of parental (childcare) leave per year, calculated by the child's birth anniversary and not multiplied by the number of children; statutory public holidays are not counted, and the leave may be taken in several parts according to law and the actual circumstances.
[D07-6.7] An only child who meets Jiangsu rules is entitled to 5 days of paid carer's leave per year while a parent aged 60 or over is in hospital because of illness; statutory public holidays are not counted, the leave may be taken in one block or in parts, and it is not carried over to the next year.
[D07-6.8] Paternity (nursing) leave, parental (childcare) leave and carer's leave for the parents of an only child are treated as attendance under the applicable rules, with wages and benefits protected. Leave for family-planning surgery and other childbirth-related leave to which the employee is entitled by law are handled under the rules in force and the medical certificate.

## 7 Protection during pregnancy and breastfeeding

[D07-7.1] Time needed for prenatal check-ups during working time counts as working time; it is not fixed at a limit of half a day a month, and it is not set off against maternity leave or annual leave.
[D07-7.2] A female employee breastfeeding a baby under 1 year old is given not less than 1 hour of breastfeeding time within each day's working time; for a multiple birth, 1 more hour a day is added for each additional baby breastfed. Time spent travelling to and from breastfeeding within the workplace counts as working time according to law.
[D07-7.3] Under Jiangsu rules, a female employee who is less than 3 months pregnant or 7 months or more pregnant is not assigned extended working hours or night shifts and is given a rest break of not less than 1 hour during each working day; a female employee breastfeeding a baby under 1 year old is not assigned extended working hours or night shifts.
[D07-7.4] Protection and treatment during menstruation, rest to protect a pregnancy, menopause, and a longer breastfeeding leave approved on application are handled according to law and the medical certificate. The company must not assign a female employee work that is prohibited for her at the relevant stage; on return to work after maternity leave, the position shall be arranged according to law and the workload restored gradually.

## 8 Work injury and other leave

[D07-8.1] An employee who needs to stop work for treatment because of a work-related accident injury or an occupational disease goes through the work-injury determination and paid work-injury leave procedures according to law. During the paid work-injury leave period, the employee's original wages and benefits remain unchanged and must not be paid at basic wage only.
[D07-8.2] The paid work-injury leave period is generally not more than 12 months; where the injury is serious or the circumstances are special, it may be extended on confirmation through the statutory procedure, as provided in the Regulations on Work-Related Injury Insurance and the applicable rules; after the period expires, treatment, assessment and benefits continue according to law.
[D07-8.3] For family-visit leave, eligibility, duration and treatment are determined by the applicable law, the employment contract, the collective contract or valid commitments the company has already made; treatment already enjoyed is not cancelled merely because of this revision. Other leave, such as for taking part in social activities according to law, is handled under the corresponding rules.
[D07-8.4] Each type of leave in this chapter is counted under its own rules; there is no single rule that leave is never extended when it coincides with a statutory public holiday. Where national or Jiangsu rules, or the rules of the place where the employment contract is performed, are changed, the change applies from the date it takes effect by law, and the Administration & HR Department promptly explains it and updates its working practice.
