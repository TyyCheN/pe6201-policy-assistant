# Chapter 3 — Employee Discipline

## 1 Day-to-day order

[D03-1.1] Employees shall communicate in a civil manner and respect colleagues and customers, and must not insult, threaten, harass or discriminate against others. Disagreements may be raised through the supervisor, the Administration & HR Department or the lawfully established trade union.
[D03-1.2] Visitors shall enter designated areas after registering as required and shall be accompanied by the person receiving them. Unrelated persons must not enter production, warehouse or other restricted areas.
[D03-1.3] Keep the work area tidy and place tools, materials and finished goods as required. Do not eat in production areas; eat only in the designated dining area. Smoking is allowed only in designated smoking areas, and open flames are strictly prohibited in no-smoking areas.
[D03-1.4] During working time employees shall concentrate on their job tasks and must not leave their post without permission, sleep on duty, gamble, work after drinking alcohol, or engage in private activities that clearly affect work or safety. An employee who needs to leave the post temporarily shall tell the supervisor and make the necessary handover.
[D03-1.5] Without authorisation, employees must not look through, copy or take other people's documents or belongings. The last person to leave shall, according to their duties, check equipment, power, doors and windows and fire exits, and must not switch off facilities that have to run continuously.

## 2 Dress and protective equipment

[D03-2.1] Dress shall be neat and meet the safety requirements of the position. Employees in production positions shall wear work clothes and work shoes as required and correctly use eye, hearing, respiratory and other protective equipment; hair, jewellery and loose clothing must be kept clear of the risk of being caught in equipment.
[D03-2.2] Slippers and other clothing unsuitable for the work are prohibited in hazardous work areas. Whether gloves must be worn or must not be worn is governed strictly by the operating procedure for the specific equipment; there is no single blanket rule.

## 3 Integrity and honesty

[D03-3.1] Employees must not use their position to solicit or accept improper benefits, and must not embezzle or misappropriate company property, accept kickbacks, submit false expense claims or help others to falsify.
[D03-3.2] Where gifts, hospitality, a relative's business relationship or a transaction interest might affect the impartial performance of duties, the employee shall promptly declare it to the supervisor and the Administration & HR Department, handle it under the company's authorisation procedure, and must not privately promise transaction terms.
[D03-3.3] Expense claims must correspond to real business, and the vouchers, amounts and approval records must be true and complete. Ordinary errors in vouchers are first verified and corrected; an error must not be classified as fraud without an investigation.

## 4 Confidentiality and information security

[D03-4.1] Employees shall protect the trade secrets of the company and its customers, including unpublished processes, drawings, die parameters, quotations, orders and customer information. These may be accessed, used and disclosed only as authorised and only to the extent necessary for performing duties.
[D03-4.2] Employees must not send materials outside the company without permission, or upload confidential materials to personal cloud drives, public platforms or artificial intelligence services that have not been approved. On leaving a post, being transferred or leaving the company, the employee shall return materials and hand over authorisations as required.
[D03-4.3] Providing materials to regulatory, judicial or arbitration bodies according to law, lawfully defending one's rights, reporting wrongdoing, or communicating with professional advisers is not a disclosure prohibited by this handbook. An employee lawfully finding out about, checking or claiming his or her own wage entitlements is not treated as a disciplinary offence; other people's pay and personal information learned through one's position must not be disclosed without permission.
[D03-4.4] An employee who discovers a stolen account, materials sent in error or any other information security incident shall report it immediately and cooperate in limiting the damage; the company's investigation shall protect privacy and be limited in scope.

## 5 Conflict of interest

[D03-5.1] Employees must not use company resources to pursue private gain that conflicts with their duties. Part-time work, business or investment activities that compete with the company's business or may affect the employee's own job shall be declared in advance and are subject to reasonable review; ordinary passive investment or normal private activity is not by itself treated as a disciplinary offence.
[D03-5.2] Where an employee also has an employment relationship with another employer and this seriously affects the completion of tasks for this company, or the employee refuses to correct it after the company raises it, the company may deal with the matter according to law. Matters involving trade secrets, intellectual property or statutory non-compete obligations are handled under the law and valid agreements.

## 6 Company property

[D03-6.1] Company property is managed through issue, custody, handover and return. Employees shall use equipment, tools and materials according to the operating requirements and must not privately lend, sell or divert them to other uses.
[D03-6.2] For any loss of property, the cause, the actual loss, fault and causation shall be established, distinguishing normal wear and tear, equipment failure, management causes and personal responsibility. Compensation at full price must not be demanded across the board; disputes are resolved by negotiation according to law or through statutory channels.

## 7 Safe production and quality

[D03-7.1] Employees must follow the operating procedures for their post covering equipment, stamping, die changing, lifting, electricity use and fire safety, and take part in safety training and emergency drills. Without authorisation they must not operate equipment, remove guards, bypass interlocks or change key parameters.
[D03-7.2] During maintenance, cleaning, troubleshooting and die changing, the machine shall be stopped, energy sources isolated and safety confirmed in accordance with the relevant procedure; it is forbidden to put any part of the body into a hazardous area that has not been safely isolated. Special operations are subject to a permit and supervision system.
[D03-7.3] An employee who discovers abnormal equipment, an accident hazard or a personal injury shall immediately stop the dangerous work, report it and take whatever safety measures are within his or her ability. Emergency rescue comes first, and the scene shall be preserved as required; concealing an incident, falsifying records or obstructing a report is prohibited.
[D03-7.4] Employees have the right to refuse instructions that violate the rules and orders to carry out risky work, and in an emergency that endangers personal safety they may lawfully stop work or evacuate; the company must not treat this as absence without leave or as a disciplinary offence.
[D03-7.5] Work shall follow the drawings, process and inspection standards, with first-piece, in-process and shift-handover records properly kept. Non-conforming products shall be labelled, segregated and reported; releasing them without authorisation, mixing materials, altering inspection results or concealing quality problems is prohibited.

## 8 Working hours and attendance

[D03-8.1] The company applies the working-hours and rest system according to law. For positions on standard working hours, normal work does not exceed 8 hours a day and 40 hours a week, and rest is guaranteed according to law. The comprehensive working-hours system and the flexible (non-fixed) working-hours system are applied only where approved according to law and only to the positions concerned.
[D03-8.2] The specific start and finish times, meal and rest breaks and shift times for each department are issued by the Administration & HR Department in a lawful shift notice based on business needs, and are made known in advance. Long opening hours at the warehouse are to be covered by shifts and do not mean that the same employee must be on duty for the whole period. An adjustment that affects what the employment contract provides is handled by agreement according to law.
[D03-8.3] Time spent in meetings, training and study sessions that the company organises and that are compulsory counts as working time according to law; where they are scheduled outside normal working hours, the overtime rules apply, and overtime is not excluded by calling the session study or a meeting.
[D03-8.4] Employees shall record their attendance truthfully and must not clock in for others or falsify records. Off-site work and business trips shall be reported in advance; employees need not make an unreasonable return trip to the company just to clock in. For an equipment failure, a missed clock-in or similar, the employee shall apply for verification and correction of the record within 1 working day of noticing it; even after that deadline the matter shall still be verified against objective records, and absence without leave is not found on the basis of the missing clock-in alone.
[D03-8.5] Lateness and early departure are recorded according to the actual time not worked; being 30 minutes or 2 hours late is not automatically converted into half a day or a full day of absence without leave. For the first two occasional late arrivals in a month, each of no more than 5 minutes, a reminder is the main response; in other cases the supervisor verifies the reason and gives a written reminder. Repeated breaches are handled under Chapter 9, and no separate fine is imposed.
[D03-8.6] Absence without leave means that an employee, without a proper reason, without approval and without being entitled by law to the corresponding leave, does not work during the time he or she is required to attend. Sudden illness, an accident, leave taken according to law, the company not having arranged work, or another reasonable cause shall be verified before any action is taken, and absence without leave is not found simply because a formality is missing.
[D03-8.7] Absence without leave is accumulated according to the actual hours of absence: a full normally scheduled working day missed counts as 1 day, a partial absence counts in proportion to the normal scheduled hours of that day, and no single day counts as more than 1 day; the total is not rounded up. Consecutive absence is counted in consecutive required attendance days, and non-working days are not counted as days of absence.
[D03-8.8] Where it is verified that an employee has, without a proper reason, been absent without leave for 3 consecutive required attendance days, or for a cumulative 3 days in the same calendar quarter, or for a cumulative 8 days in the same calendar year, this may be treated as a serious breach of discipline; the company must investigate, send a formal reminder, hear the employee's explanation and decide according to law whether to terminate, as provided in Chapter 9, and termination is not automatic.
[D03-8.9] Attendance is recorded by the department, checked by the employee and reviewed by the Administration & HR Department before being passed to Finance for payroll. An employee who disagrees with a record may submit evidence and apply for a correction; attendance and wage payment records are kept for at least 2 years according to law.

## 9 Overtime

[D03-9.1] Overtime shall be based on a real business need and go through application, approval and confirmation of the hours actually worked, with consultation according to law and within the statutory limits on hours. Where, in special circumstances, a supervisor arranges overtime first, it shall be recorded promptly afterwards.
[D03-9.2] For extended hours on a working day, for work on a rest day where compensatory time off cannot be arranged according to law, and for work on a statutory public holiday, the company pays according to law not less than 150%, 200% and 300% respectively of the corresponding wage base. Overtime on a statutory public holiday must not be compensated with time off alone in place of overtime pay.
[D03-9.3] Staying on without approval does not automatically count as overtime, but work that the company actually arranged, required or accepted shall be verified from evidence such as the task, communications and attendance records, and actual overtime must not be denied merely because an approval form is missing.
[D03-9.4] Liability for safety, work injury and damage during overtime is determined according to law, and the company's liability must not be excluded across the board on the ground that the overtime was not approved or that the incident happened after working hours.

## 10 Leave requests and business trip reporting

[D03-10.1] The conditions, approval, late filing, extension and return-from-leave reporting for leave are all governed by Chapter 7; other chapters do not set separate time limits or penalties.
[D03-10.2] A business trip shall be applied for in advance; for an urgent trip, the employee reports first and completes the paperwork within 3 working days after returning to the company. Real business activity arising from the company's arrangement is not treated directly as absence without leave because the paperwork was completed late.
