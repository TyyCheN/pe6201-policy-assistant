# Chapter 9 — Rewards and Discipline

## 1 Basic principles

[D09-1.1] Rewards and disciplinary action shall be based on facts and on valid rules that were made known in advance, taking into account the nature of the conduct, fault, consequences, correction and repetition; the severity of the action shall be proportionate to the circumstances, and subjective evaluation or a single label must not take the place of evidence.
[D09-1.2] The same facts are not punished twice with the same kind of action; combining different measures requires a lawful basis, and they are not counted twice towards cumulative disciplinary records. The company does not impose punitive fines, and does not use arbitrary pay cuts, stopping social insurance or unpaid suspension as disciplinary action.
[D09-1.3] Taking leave according to law, reporting wrongdoing, filing a grievance, taking part in trade union activities, demanding payment of remuneration or refusing instructions that violate the rules is not a ground for discipline or retaliation.

## 2 Rewards

[D09-2.1] Employees with outstanding work results, or who contribute to safety and quality improvement, technical innovation, constructive suggestions, saving resources or protecting the company's lawful interests, may be given commendation, a bonus, development or promotion opportunities.
[D09-2.2] A reward is proposed by the department with the supporting facts and announced after approval. The specific amount and conditions follow the published special scheme or a written decision; a general reward clause is not to be read as a commitment to a fixed amount.

## 3 Levels of disciplinary action

[D09-3.1] Depending on the circumstances, the following may be used: verbal coaching and reminder, written warning, demerit and major demerit; where the statutory conditions for termination are met, the employment contract is terminated according to law. Unless the nature or consequences are already serious, an opportunity to correct shall be given first.
[D09-3.2] For minor, first-time lapses in hygiene, order or reporting, or work errors, that have not caused any clear adverse consequence, verbal coaching and a reminder are used, with the correction required explained.
[D09-3.3] For minor breaches that recur after a reminder, or for breaching a reasonable work instruction without a proper reason, leaving the post without permission, sleeping on duty or disturbing work order, where the conduct is not yet serious, a written warning may be given; where the facts, fault and consequences call for a heavier measure, a demerit or major demerit may be given, with the basis stated in writing.
[D09-3.4] Where a loss is caused, the matter is handled in light of intent or negligence, the actual consequences and the responsibilities of the position; the level of disciplinary action must not be fixed mechanically by the amount of the loss, and an ordinary work mistake is not equated with serious dereliction of duty.
[D09-3.5] Disciplinary actions over the whole life of the employment contract are not accumulated without limit as a basis for automatic termination. Repeated breaches may be a factor in judging the circumstances, but the number of disciplinary actions does not by itself replace the review of seriousness, of the validity of the rules and of the statutory conditions for termination.

## 4 Serious breaches and review of termination

[D09-4.1] Where any of the following conduct is verified and amounts to a serious breach of valid company rules, or meets another statutory condition for termination, the company may terminate the employment contract according to law; being listed in this section does not by itself relieve the company of its burden of proof and its procedural obligations.
[D09-4.2] It is verified that the employee has reached the standard of absence without leave in Chapter 3, clause 8.8, without a proper reason, and still cannot give a reasonable explanation after reasonable contact, a formal reminder and a hearing of the employee's account.
[D09-4.3] Intentionally stealing, embezzling or misappropriating the property of the company or of others, accepting kickbacks, submitting false expense claims or colluding to harm the company's interests, where the circumstances are serious.
[D09-4.4] Intentionally disclosing, stealing or unlawfully providing protected trade secrets, causing major damage or a serious risk of disclosure; intentionally forging key business, safety, quality or financial documents or signatures, seriously damaging trust in management and transactions.
[D09-4.5] Intentionally damaging equipment, dies or safety facilities, removing guards or bypassing interlocks without authorisation, or working in violation of the rules, causing a serious accident or a major real danger; or refusing to stop dangerous conduct after being told to stop.
[D09-4.6] Violent assault, serious threats, sexual harassment or other serious violation of another person's personal rights; in making the finding, self-defence and stopping a danger shall be distinguished from active aggression, and an ordinary argument or reasonable criticism is not a ground for direct termination.
[D09-4.7] Refusing a lawful and reasonable work arrangement without a proper reason and still refusing to correct after a written explanation, communication and a request to correct, seriously affecting production or work order; where a contract change, a health restriction or an instruction that violates the rules is involved, whether the arrangement itself is lawful and reasonable shall be reviewed first.
[D09-4.8] Serious dereliction of duty or graft causing major damage to the company; having an employment relationship with another employer at the same time that seriously affects the employee's own job, or refusing to correct this after it is raised; being held criminally liable according to law; and other cases in which the law allows termination, are each reviewed against the corresponding statutory requirements.
[D09-4.9] Not being competent for the job, the expiry of the medical treatment period and other non-disciplinary terminations do not follow the disciplinary procedure as a substitute; the statutory conditions shall be met, including training or a change of position, notice, severance pay and the protective provisions.

## 5 Investigation and decision procedure

[D09-5.1] The department puts forward the facts and evidence, and the Administration & HR Department verifies when the conduct occurred, whether the rule had been made known, the employee's explanation, witnesses and objective records. The employee shall be told the facts in question and given a reasonable opportunity to state his or her case and defend himself or herself.
[D09-5.2] Where absence without leave or loss of contact is suspected, the company shall try to make contact by reasonable means, such as the telephone number, messages, email and contact address on file, send a notice requiring an explanation or a return to work and keep evidence of delivery, first ruling out reasonable causes such as sudden illness or an accident.
[D09-5.3] Disciplinary action is reviewed by management personnel with the authority to do so; where termination of the employment contract is proposed, the legal basis, the procedure and any special protection shall be reviewed and the termination approved by the General Manager or an authorised person. Where a trade union has been established, the union is notified in advance according to law and its opinion considered.
[D09-5.4] The written decision shall set out the main facts, the evidence, the clauses applied, the outcome and the grievance channel, and shall be delivered according to law; where the employee refuses to sign, delivery is proved by lawful means, and the refusal to sign is not by itself treated as a new serious breach.
[D09-5.5] The company may make reasonable temporary adjustments to work arrangements for the purposes of the investigation and safety, but must not use this to impose unpaid suspension. Public notice shall be limited to what is necessary for management, shall not disclose unrelated personal information and shall not insult the employee.

## 6 Grievances

[D09-6.1] An employee may file a written grievance with the Administration & HR Department or the General Manager within 5 working days of receiving a disciplinary action; where the person who made the original decision is involved in the dispute, the review shall as far as possible be assigned to someone else.
[D09-6.2] The company in principle replies within 10 working days, and for complex matters explains the reason for any extension. Where the facts or the basis are found to be wrong, this shall be corrected promptly and any treatment payable by law made up.
[D09-6.3] An internal grievance does not restrict the right to apply for mediation, labour arbitration, litigation or a labour inspection complaint according to law, and does not change the statutory limitation periods; an employee must not be required to accept the disciplinary action as a precondition for receiving wages or the leaving certificate.
